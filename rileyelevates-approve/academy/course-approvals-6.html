Denied with a good score? Here's why

A denial with a good score is frustrating, but it's rarely random. It's usually one of a short list of reasons.

THE MOST COMMON REASONS
Too many recent inquiries: several applications in a short time.
Thin or young file: few accounts, or nothing seasoned past a couple of years.
A recent new account: something opened in the last few months.
Income or DTI: the payment doesn't fit the documented income.
High or rising utilization: balances high on statement date.
A recent negative: one new late payment can outweigh a good score.
Lender-specific rules: too many new accounts overall, or too many with that lender.
Verification problems: the information on the application couldn't be confirmed.

HOW TO READ AN ADVERSE ACTION NOTICE
When you're denied, the lender must tell you, usually in writing. The notice should list the main reasons, and if your credit report was used, which bureau supplied it and your right to a free copy of that report within 60 days. Read the reasons carefully; they tell you exactly what to work on.

WHAT TO DO NEXT
1. Get the free report from the bureau named in the notice, and check it for errors.
2. Match each reason to a fix: wait out inquiries, let new accounts season, lower balances before the statement date, lower DTI.
3. Don't immediately apply somewhere else. That adds another inquiry to the same file.
4. Some lenders have a reconsideration line. If you have new information, such as income that wasn't included, you can ask politely.
