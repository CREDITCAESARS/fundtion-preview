Bankruptcy reporting

A bankruptcy shows up in two places on your report: the public record entry, and the individual accounts that were included in it. Both need to be accurate.

HOW LONG EACH CHAPTER CAN REPORT
Chapter 7: up to 10 years from the filing date.
Chapter 13: the law allows up to 10 years; the major bureaus generally remove a Chapter 13 7 years from the filing date.
Check the dates on your report. Once the period has passed, the record should be gone.

CHECK THE PUBLIC RECORD ENTRY
Chapter, filing date, discharge date, case number and court. Compare them to your discharge papers. Bureaus usually get this information from public record vendors, not from the court itself, so errors happen. Dispute any detail that is wrong and attach your court documents.

INCLUDED ACCOUNTS SHOULD REPORT AS DISCHARGED
Every account included in the bankruptcy should show a zero balance and a status such as "included in bankruptcy" or "discharged". Common errors:
an included account still showing a balance or as past due;
late payments reported after the filing date;
a collector reporting a discharged debt as if it were still owed.
Get your list of creditors from your bankruptcy papers, check each one on all three bureaus, and dispute any that are wrong with the discharge order attached.

REBUILDING AFTER DISCHARGE
Start small: a secured card or a credit-builder account, paid on time every month, with low balances. Your on-time history after discharge is what lenders look at next. Never pay anyone who promises to remove an accurate bankruptcy.
