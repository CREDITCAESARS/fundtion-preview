Clean up your personal information

Start with the top of the report, not the accounts. The personal information section is how a bureau decides which records belong to you.

WHAT TO CHECK ON EACH BUREAU
Your name and any spelling variations.
Current and previous addresses.
Date of birth and the partial Social Security number.
Employers.
Phone numbers.

WHAT TO CORRECT
Correct anything that is genuinely wrong: a misspelled name, an address you never lived at, an employer you never worked for. Old addresses where you really lived are normal and are not errors.

WHAT NEVER TO DO
Never add made-up name variations or remove correct addresses to make the bureau's matching fail. That is a misleading statement, and it can create bigger problems than the ones you started with.

HOW TO REQUEST CORRECTIONS
Write one short letter to each bureau that shows the error. List the items that are wrong and what the correct information is. Attach a copy of a government ID and a proof of address dated in the last 60 days, such as a utility bill or bank statement. Mail it with tracking and keep a copy.

WHY THIS COMES FIRST
When your identifying information is clean, later disputes are matched to the right file, and mixed-file problems (a relative with the same name, an old roommate's account) are easier to spot.

HOMEWORK
Highlight every wrong personal-information entry on each bureau and draft your Round 1 letters.
