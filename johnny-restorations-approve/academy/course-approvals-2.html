What underwriting checks besides your score

Here's the checklist behind the curtain. The order below is roughly how most lenders think about it.

1. CAPACITY
Can you afford the payment? Your documented income and your debt-to-income ratio. The next lesson covers DTI.

2. DEPTH OF FILE
How many accounts you have and how long you've had them. A thin file (only a few accounts, or nothing older than about two years) can be declined at a score that would approve a thicker file.

3. SEASONING
How old your newest account is. Many lenders prefer nothing opened in the last several months.

4. INQUIRIES
How many times you've applied recently. Many applications in a short time read as risk.

5. RECENT NEGATIVES
Recent problems weigh more than old ones. A late payment last month matters more than a collection from years ago.

6. UTILIZATION AND ITS DIRECTION
Not just how much of your limits you use, but whether balances are climbing month after month.

7. STABILITY
Time at your job and your address.

8. RELATIONSHIP
Whether you already bank with the lender. An existing relationship can help.

Capacity, stability and relationship aren't on your credit report at all.

WHICH ONES YOU CAN FIX FASTEST
Utilization can change within one statement cycle. Inquiries and seasoning improve with time and patience. Depth of file takes the longest.

STATED INCOME MUST BE TRUE INCOME
Report only income you actually have and can document if asked. Inflating income on a credit application is a serious problem and can be a crime. A lower honest number is always the right number.
