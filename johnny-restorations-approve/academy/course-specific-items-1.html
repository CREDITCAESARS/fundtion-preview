Late payments: accuracy and goodwill

Late payments are among the most damaging items on a report, and the most recent ones hurt most. There are two different situations, and they need two different approaches.

1. THE LATE PAYMENT IS WRONG
Check the dates and amounts against your own records: bank statements, payment confirmations, autopay history. Common errors include a payment you made within the grace period reported as late, a late reported for a month the account was closed or paid, or different late months on different bureaus. If you can prove it, dispute it with the bureau and the lender, with the proof attached.

2. THE LATE PAYMENT IS RIGHT
If you really paid late, an accuracy dispute is not the tool. Do not claim a hardship arrangement or other accommodation that was never granted.

A goodwill request is the honest option. It is a short, polite letter to the lender asking it to consider removing an accurate late payment as a courtesy. Include:
how long you've been a customer and your payment record since;
what happened, briefly and truthfully;
what you've changed so it won't happen again.
The lender doesn't have to agree, and many won't. It costs nothing to ask.

AUTOPAY SO IT NEVER HAPPENS AGAIN
Set every account to pay at least the minimum automatically, then pay the rest by hand. One new late payment can undo months of progress.

HOW LONG LATES REPORT
Up to 7 years, but their weight fades as they age and as newer on-time history builds.
