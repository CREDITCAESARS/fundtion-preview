Relationship banking

Banks often treat existing customers differently from strangers. A deposit relationship can come before the application, and it can help the application.

WHY IT HELPS
A bank where you already have checking and savings can see your deposits, your balances and how you manage money. That information isn't on your credit report, and it can support an application.

OPENING ACCOUNTS FIRST
Open a checking or savings account with the bank you would like to borrow from, for yourself or your business. Use it for real: direct deposit, regular activity, a healthy balance. Don't open it the same day you apply for credit.

SEASONING
Give the relationship time before you apply. Many lenders look for months of history, not days. The exact amount of time differs by bank and isn't usually published.

TALKING TO A BANKER
Ask to meet a banker, especially for business accounts. Ask:
what they look for before offering a card or a line;
which products existing customers can pre-qualify for;
whether they offer secured or deposit-backed products;
what records they want for a business application.
Bring organized records. A banker who knows you can explain your file to the people who decide.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Don't move money in and out just to make balances look bigger, and never misstate income or business revenue. Banks verify, and a misstatement can close the relationship for good.
