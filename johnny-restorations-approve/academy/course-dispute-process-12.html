Filing a CFPB complaint

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau takes complaints about credit reporting and debt collection at consumerfinance.gov/complaint. Filing is free and you do not need a lawyer.

WHEN A COMPLAINT MAKES SENSE
After you have disputed in writing and the problem is still there: an error that keeps being verified, a missed deadline, a reinsertion without notice, a collector that kept reporting a disputed debt without noting the dispute. A complaint backed by a paper trail is far stronger than one filed cold.

HOW TO WRITE IT
Stick to facts and dates: what is wrong, when you disputed it, how it was sent, what came back.
Name the specific error. Not "this account is wrong", but exactly which field is wrong and why.
File a separate complaint against each company involved: each bureau, and the company that reports the account.
Choose the right category: credit reporting for bureau problems, debt collection for collectors.

ATTACH YOUR PAPER TRAIL
Your dispute letters, delivery proof, the responses you received and the report pages. Remove anything you don't need to share, such as full account numbers.

WHAT TO EXPECT BACK
The company is asked to respond, typically within about 15 days, and can take longer for a final answer. Read the response carefully. If it doesn't address what you raised, you can say so in your feedback.

IF A COMPANY OFFERS A DEAL
Get any agreement in writing first, and check your reports to confirm the change before you close anything out.

Everything you file must be true and must be your own facts.
