Lender rules of thumb

Beyond your score and DTI, many lenders run internal rules. Break one and the application can be declined no matter how good the rest of the file is. Most of these rules are not published, they change over time, and they differ from lender to lender. Treat them as patterns to ask about, not guarantees.

RECENT-ACCOUNT LIMITS
Some large card issuers are widely reported to decline applicants who opened several new personal cards (from any lender) in the last 24 months. Count your new accounts from the last two years before you apply.

APPLICATION VELOCITY
Many issuers limit how many of their own cards you can open in a short window, for example one or two new cards in a 60 to 90 day period. Some also limit how many total accounts you can hold with them.

BUREAU PREFERENCE
Lenders pull different bureaus, and sometimes different bureaus in different states. Some pull more than one. If one bureau is much cleaner than the others, which lender you pick matters.

WHY ORDER MATTERS
Every application adds an inquiry, and each approval adds a new account. Both make the next application slightly harder. So the order of your applications matters as much as which ones you choose.

WHAT TO DO WITH THIS
1. Count your new accounts in the last 24 months.
2. Before applying, ask the lender or check its pre-qualification tool, which is often a soft pull.
3. Don't apply to several lenders the same week hoping one says yes.
4. Make a written plan: which products, in which order, and when you will stop.
