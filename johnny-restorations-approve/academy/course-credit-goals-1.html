Buying a home

A mortgage is the most thorough review your file will ever get. Prepare for it months ahead.

WHAT MORTGAGE LENDERS REVIEW
Credit from all three bureaus. Mortgage lenders often use older score versions than the ones in free apps, so your mortgage scores can differ from what you're used to seeing.
Debt-to-income, including the new housing payment.
Income and job history, usually the last two years.
Assets: down payment, closing costs and reserves.
Explanations for recent inquiries, new accounts or large deposits.

MORTGAGE SCORE VERSIONS
Ask your loan officer which scores they use and what minimums apply to the loan programs you're considering. Programs differ in what they require.

DOWN PAYMENT AND RESERVES
Save for the down payment, closing costs and a cushion. Keep the money in your own account and keep records of where it came from. Lenders ask about large deposits they can't trace.

GET PRE-APPROVED
A pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your credit, income and assets. It tells you your price range before you shop, and sellers take it seriously.

DON'T OPEN ANYTHING DURING UNDERWRITING
From pre-approval to closing, don't apply for new credit, finance furniture or a car, change jobs without talking to your lender, or make large unexplained deposits. Lenders often re-check credit before closing.

HOMEWORK
Calculate your DTI with the new housing payment included, and talk to a loan officer or a HUD-approved housing counselor about which programs fit you.
