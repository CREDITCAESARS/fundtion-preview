Dispute directly with the furnisher

The furnisher is the company that reports the account: the lender, the card issuer or the collector. It has its own legal duty to investigate disputes, separate from the bureau's.

WHY THIS ROUND MATTERS
A bureau dispute is often handled electronically. A direct dispute goes to the company that holds the records, and federal rules (Regulation V, 12 C.F.R. 1022.43) require it to investigate a direct dispute that meets the requirements.

FINDING THE FURNISHER'S DISPUTE ADDRESS
Check the account entry on your report, the company's monthly statements or website, or the method-of-verification answer from the bureau. Many companies list a specific address for credit reporting disputes. Use that one.

WHAT TO SEND
Your name, address and the account number.
The specific information you believe is wrong, and why.
Copies of supporting documents: statements, payment records, letters.
Keep the same facts you sent the bureau. Consistency matters.

WHAT TO KEEP
A copy of everything, the certified mail receipt and the date delivered. Put it on your tracking sheet as its own row.

HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM A BUREAU DISPUTE
A bureau dispute asks the bureau to check with the furnisher. A direct dispute asks the furnisher to check its own records. You can do both for the same item, as long as each one names a real error.

A NOTE ON REPEATS
Sending the same dispute again and again without new information can be treated as frivolous. Each round should add something: a new document, a new specific error, or the answer you received.
