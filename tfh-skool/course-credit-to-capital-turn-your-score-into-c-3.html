Bridge Loans & DSCR Financing

Video lives in Najaa's live group (not copied into the preview)

From Najaa's live lesson

Two financing tools every investor needs in their toolbox. By the end, you'll know what they are, when to use each one, and how they work together to fund a fix and flip or a buy and hold.

Part 1: What Is a Bridge Loan

A bridge loan is short term financing that gets you from point A to point B on a deal.

Think of it as the loan that bridges the gap between buying a property today and either selling it or refinancing it later. Terms typically run 6 to 24 months. Approval is based more on the deal and the property than on your personal income, which is why investors lean on bridge loans instead of trying to qualify through a traditional bank.

How it works for a fix and flip:

You find a distressed property. A bridge loan lender funds the purchase and often the renovation budget too, based on the after repair value (ARV), not just the current condition of the house. You fix it up, then either sell it for a profit or refinance into long term financing.

Why investors use it:

Speed is the biggest reason. Bridge loans close in days or weeks, not the 30 to 45 days a conventional mortgage takes. Sellers of distressed properties want certainty and speed, and a bridge loan lets you compete like a cash buyer. Bridge loans also fund the rehab budget, so you're not draining your own cash to cover materials and labor.

The tradeoff:

Interest rates run higher than a conventional loan, usually in the 9 to 13 percent range, and the loan is short term by design. This isn't the financing you hold for 10 years. It's the financing that gets the deal done so you can move to the next stage.

Part 2: What Is a DSCR Loan
