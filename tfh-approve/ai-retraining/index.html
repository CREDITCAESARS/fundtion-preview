What we found

Your older text assistant answered texts that were meant for you, not for clients. It thanked a rewards-points alert as if it were you, and it 'agreed' to forward setup steps that were sent to you as the owner.
Some answers were out of date or promised too much. It quoted October sale prices that have since changed. Its instructions still offer 'double your money back'. It also promised 'my team will text you within the hour' and offered to send client results as proof.
A client got the wrong answer about the $49.95 monitoring fee. The assistant said their program had ended and they had to buy a new package, which wasn't what you had told them. You had to call to clear it up.
The new assistants sometimes talked over you. Three times, an assistant jumped in while you were already talking with that person yourself. Clients got lead messages too: one who had just paid got a 'not booked yet' text, and one who was thanking you got the first lead question.
The READY auto-reply fires too easily. It goes out when a text just contains 'ready', 'sale' or 'free', even 'I'm not ready right now' or a billing question, sometimes twice, and it says 'it won't last long'.
Phone calls: four things to fix. Hope switched to Spanish on her own and kept going after the caller said no Spanish. A transfer to the team went to voicemail on a Friday evening. Hope asks two questions at once. When callers say you sent them, she mishears your name. One more: three people calling back after a missed call from the team hung up on the care line's greeting within seconds.
A few messages got no answer. Eight conversations ended with a message nobody answered, including a question your assistant had no answer for: wage garnishments.
Small polish. The bot texts use a key emoji instead of your heart. The first text has two opt-out lines. One 'not booked yet' text printed a name in ALL CAPS. The booking text doesn't say the time is the person's local time.
