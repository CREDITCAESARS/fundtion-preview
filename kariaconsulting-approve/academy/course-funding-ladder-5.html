Which bureau a lender pulls

Lenders don't all pull the same bureau. That makes the order of your applications a real strategy.

WHY IT MATTERS
Each hard inquiry lands on the bureau that was pulled. If your best bureau takes several inquiries early, it looks weaker for the applications you care about most. And if one bureau has an error the others don't, a lender that pulls that bureau will see a worse file.

HOW TO FIND OUT
Ask the lender which bureau it typically pulls for that product in your state.
Read the adverse action or approval letter after any application; it names the bureau used.
Look at your own reports: the inquiry section shows who pulled which bureau.
Patterns change and can differ by state and product, so confirm before you rely on them.

MAPPING YOUR APPLICATIONS BY BUREAU
Make a simple table: lender, product, which bureau, your score and inquiry count on that bureau. Spread inquiries so no single bureau takes all of them, and put the applications you care about most where your file is strongest.

PRE-QUALIFICATION IS USUALLY A SOFT PULL
Many lenders offer a pre-qualification or pre-approval check that doesn't affect your score. It isn't an approval, but it can tell you whether to apply. Read the screen carefully: some pre-qualification offers turn into a hard pull once you accept.

PROTECTING YOUR BEST BUREAU
Save your strongest bureau for your highest-priority application. Don't spend it on a store card or a low-value approval.
