When to talk to a consumer attorney

Some problems need a lawyer, not another letter. Knowing when to make that call is part of doing this right.

SIGNS OF A POSSIBLE REPORTING VIOLATION
An error you proved in writing that keeps being verified anyway.
An item that came back after deletion with no notice.
A mixed file: accounts of another person on your report that won't come off.
A collector reporting a debt you disputed without noting the dispute, or contacting you in ways the law limits.
Real harm you can document, such as a denial that cites the error.

BEING SUED ON A DEBT: NEVER IGNORE IT
If you are served with a lawsuit, the deadline to respond is short. Missing it can lead to a default judgment, which can allow wage garnishment or a bank levy. Talk to a consumer attorney or your local legal aid office right away.

HOW CONSUMER ATTORNEYS ARE OFTEN PAID
The credit reporting and debt collection laws allow a court to order the company to pay attorney fees when a consumer wins. Because of that, many consumer attorneys offer a free consultation, and some take strong cases without charging you upfront. Ask how they are paid before you start.

BRING YOUR PAPER TRAIL TO THE CONSULT
Your folder: every letter, every response, delivery proof, report pages before and after, and your tracking sheet. The better your records, the faster an attorney can tell you whether you have a case.

Nobody can promise that you have a case or what it is worth. That is exactly what the consult is for.
