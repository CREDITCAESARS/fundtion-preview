ChexSystems and banking reports

If a bank won't open an account for you, the reason is often a banking report, not your credit report. ChexSystems and Early Warning Services are consumer reporting agencies that banks use for checking and savings accounts.

WHAT THEY HOLD
Accounts closed with a negative balance, unpaid overdraft fees, suspected fraud, and similar banking history. Entries generally stay for up to 5 years.

REQUESTING YOUR CHEXSYSTEMS REPORT
You can request your consumer file free once every 12 months, and also after a bank denies you because of it. If a bank turns you down, it must tell you which company's report it used. Request the report from that company's official consumer website.

DISPUTING INACCURATE ENTRIES
Read every entry: the bank, the amount, the date, the reason code. If something is wrong (an amount you already paid, an account that isn't yours, a fee you can prove was reversed), dispute it in writing with the reporting company and the bank, with proof attached. The same investigation rules apply as with credit bureaus.

IF THE ENTRY IS ACCURATE
If you really owe the bank, contacting the bank directly is often better than a dispute. If you agree to pay, get the agreement in writing before you pay, including how the entry will be updated. Then request your file again to confirm.

SECOND-CHANCE ACCOUNTS WHILE YOU WORK ON IT
Many banks and credit unions offer second-chance checking accounts designed for people with a banking report entry. They may have a monthly fee or limits. Use one to rebuild a clean banking history while you resolve the entry.
