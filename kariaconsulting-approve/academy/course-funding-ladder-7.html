Credit unions

Credit unions are member-owned financial institutions. They can be more flexible than big banks, especially for people who are rebuilding.

ELIGIBILITY ROUTES
Every credit union has membership rules. Common routes:
where you live or work;
your employer or industry;
family members who are already members;
joining a partner association or making a small donation to an affiliated organization.
Each credit union lists its current routes on its own website. These routes change, so always check the credit union's own eligibility page before relying on any list you find online.

ONE PULL, SEVERAL PRODUCTS
Some credit unions let one credit check count for more than one product within a short window, for example a card and an auto loan. This is a policy of individual credit unions, not a rule. Ask how long a credit check stays valid for other products before you apply.

WHY THEY CAN BE MORE FLEXIBLE
Credit unions often consider your whole relationship: deposits, how long you've been a member, your direct deposit. Many offer credit-builder loans and secured cards. Some also offer limit increases without a hard pull. Ask how they handle it.

LOCAL VS NATIONAL
Local credit unions may know your community and offer personal service. National ones may have wider membership and online tools. A good approach is to join one and build a relationship before you need credit: open a savings account, set up direct deposit, and let it season.
