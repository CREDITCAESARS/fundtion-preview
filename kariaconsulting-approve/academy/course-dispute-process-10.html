Collections: debt validation

When a debt collector contacts you, the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act gives you the right to ask it to validate the debt.

THE VALIDATION WINDOW
A collector must send you a validation notice with the amount, the name of the creditor and your dispute rights. If you dispute the debt in writing within 30 days of receiving that notice, the collector must stop collecting until it sends you verification. That is why this window matters: use it.

WHAT A VALIDATION REQUEST ASKS FOR
A short letter, sent by certified mail, that:
names the collector and the reference number;
says you dispute the debt and request validation;
asks for the amount, how it was calculated, the name of the original creditor and who owns the debt now.

Keep it to facts. Never admit or promise anything in this letter.

WHAT VALIDATION DOES NOT DO
Validation is a lower bar than many people think. A collector can often satisfy it by confirming the amount and the creditor. It is a step in the process, and it does not by itself remove anything.

PAYING A COLLECTION: WHAT IT DOES AND DOESN'T DO
Paying changes the balance, not the history. A paid collection can still report.
Paying does not restart the credit reporting clock. That runs from the date the account first went delinquent.
In many states a payment or a written promise to pay can restart the statute of limitations for being sued. Check your state's rules before paying an old debt.
If you settle, get the agreement in writing before any money moves. Forgiven amounts can sometimes be taxable.

NEVER IGNORE A LAWSUIT
If you are served with court papers about a debt, respond by the deadline. Missing it can lead to a default judgment.
