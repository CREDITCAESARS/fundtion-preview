Your dispute road map

A dispute is a written request asking a credit bureau, or the company that reports an account, to correct information that is inaccurate, incomplete or cannot be verified. That is all it is, and that is all it should ever be.

WHAT A DISPUTE IS NOT
It is not a way to remove accurate information you don't like. It is not a form letter that claims an account isn't yours when it is. Sending untrue statements to a bureau or a lender can hurt you, and nobody should advise you to do it.

HOW THE ROUNDS FIT TOGETHER
Round 1: clean up your personal information so the bureaus are matching the right file.
Round 2: dispute specific, provable errors with each bureau that shows them. At the same time you can dispute directly with the company reporting the account.
Round 3: when an item comes back "verified", ask how it was verified.
Round 4 and later: escalate the lawful way, with a written complaint and, where the record supports it, a consumer attorney.

WHY ABOUT 30 DAYS PER ROUND
A bureau generally has 30 days to investigate your dispute (up to 45 if you send more information during the window). Sending the next round before the results are back mixes up your paper trail and can get later letters treated as repeats.

YOUR FOLDER
Before you send anything, make one folder (paper or digital). Every letter you send, every response you get, every envelope, tracking number and date goes in it. The folder is what makes Round 3 and Round 4 possible.

HOMEWORK
Pull your three reports, list every item you believe is wrong, and next to each one write one sentence: what exactly is wrong with it. If you can't write that sentence, it goes on a separate "not disputable" list for now.
