Buying a car

Get your financing before you get to the dealership. It changes the whole conversation.

CREDIT UNION PRE-APPROVAL
Banks and credit unions offer auto loan pre-approvals. A pre-approval tells you the amount and rate you qualify for, so you can compare it to dealer financing instead of accepting the first offer. Credit unions are often competitive on auto loans, especially for members.

RATE SHOPPING WINDOWS
Common credit scoring models count multiple auto loan inquiries within a short period as one inquiry for scoring purposes, because they recognize you're shopping for one loan. The window depends on the scoring model. Do your rate shopping within a couple of weeks, not spread over months.

KNOW YOUR NUMBERS FIRST
Your budget for the total price, not just the monthly payment.
Your down payment.
The loan term. Longer terms lower the payment but cost more in total and can leave you owing more than the car is worth.

READING THE BUYER'S ORDER
Before you sign, read every line:
the vehicle price;
fees, taxes and registration;
add-ons such as extended warranties, gap coverage and service plans, which are usually optional;
the interest rate, term and total amount financed.
Compare it to your pre-approval. If something was added that you didn't ask for, ask for it to be removed.

AFTER YOU BUY
Set up automatic payments. An auto loan paid on time builds installment history on your file.
