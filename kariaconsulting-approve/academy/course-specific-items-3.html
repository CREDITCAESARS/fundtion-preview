Evictions and rental history

Landlords usually don't just pull your credit report. They often use tenant screening reports, which come from specialty consumer reporting agencies. Those companies are covered by the Fair Credit Reporting Act too.

WHERE RENTAL HISTORY SHOWS UP
Tenant screening reports: past evictions, court filings, rental payment history.
Credit reports: unpaid rent or damages that went to a collection agency can appear as a collection.

REQUESTING YOUR RENTAL HISTORY REPORT
If a landlord turns you down because of a screening report, it must tell you which company supplied it. You can request a copy of your file from that company. Many screening companies also give one free file disclosure each year on request. Read it like a credit report: names, addresses, cases, dates, amounts.

DISPUTING ERRORS WITH THE SCREENING COMPANY
Common errors: a case that belongs to someone with a similar name, a case that was dismissed or that you won but shows as an eviction, wrong amounts, or very old records. Dispute in writing with the screening company and attach court records that show the correct outcome. The same investigation rules apply.

JUDGMENTS VS FILINGS
An eviction filing is not the same as an eviction judgment. A case can be filed and then dismissed or settled. Ask the court clerk for the case record so you know exactly what happened, and make sure the report says the same.

UNPAID RENT IN COLLECTIONS
Treat it like any other collection: check the amount and dates, use the debt validation window, and get any settlement in writing.
