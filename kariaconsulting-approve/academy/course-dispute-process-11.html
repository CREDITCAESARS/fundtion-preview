Consumer law reference library

These are the federal sections that come up most. Reference only, in plain English.

FAIR CREDIT REPORTING ACT
15 U.S.C. 1681b, permissible purpose: who may pull your report and why.
1681c, how long items can report: most negatives up to 7 years from the date of first delinquency; Chapter 7 bankruptcy up to 10 years.
1681e(b), accuracy: bureaus must follow reasonable procedures for maximum possible accuracy.
1681g, your file: your right to see what is in your file. This is the real "609".
1681i, disputes: the bureau investigates, generally within 30 days, and you can ask how an item was verified.
1681s-2, furnishers: companies that report must report accurately and investigate disputes.

FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES ACT
15 U.S.C. 1692c, contact limits: no calls at inconvenient times and a right to stop contact in writing.
1692e, no false or misleading statements, including reporting a disputed debt without noting the dispute.
1692g, validation: the 30-day window to dispute in writing.
It applies to debt collectors, not usually to the original lender.

FAIR CREDIT BILLING ACT
15 U.S.C. 1666: billing-error disputes on credit cards, generally within 60 days of the statement.

KEY CLOCKS
Bureau investigation: 30 days (45 with added documents).
Method-of-verification description: 15 days.
Reinsertion notice: 5 business days.
Debt validation request: 30 days from the validation notice.

YOUR STATE MAY ADD PROTECTIONS
Many states have their own credit reporting and debt collection laws. Your state attorney general's website is a good place to start.
