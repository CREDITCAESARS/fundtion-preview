Collections and charge-offs

A charge-off means the original lender wrote the account off as a loss. A collection means a debt collector is trying to collect it, either for the lender or because it bought the debt. One debt can show up both ways.

ORIGINAL CREDITOR VS COLLECTOR
Find out who reports what. The original creditor and a collector can both appear for the same debt, but only one of them should show a balance owed.

WHAT TO CHECK
Balance: does the balance match across bureaus and between the creditor and the collector?
Dates: is the date of first delinquency the same everywhere, and does it match when you actually fell behind?
Activity after charge-off: payment history that keeps updating month after month after the charge-off date.
Duplicates: the same debt reported by two different collectors.
Age: is the item older than 7 years from the date of first delinquency?

Each of these is a specific, provable kind of error. Dispute only what is actually wrong.

YOUR OPTIONS WHEN IT ALL CHECKS OUT
If the item is accurate, you can still decide whether and how to resolve the debt. Read the debt validation lesson first, and check your state's statute of limitations before paying an old debt.

SETTLING: GET IT IN WRITING FIRST
If you negotiate, get the full agreement in writing before you pay: the amount, the payment date and exactly how the account will be reported afterwards. A verbal promise is worth nothing. After you pay, check your reports to confirm the change.
