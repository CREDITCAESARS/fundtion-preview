Builder loans and secured cards

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
What a primary account is, and how credit builder loans and secured cards work.

PRIMARY ACCOUNTS
A primary account is one where YOU are the account holder: you applied, you were approved and you're responsible for the debt. On your report the responsibility field says Individual. History on your own accounts stays with you, which is why primaries are the foundation of a profile.

A WARNING ABOUT "PRIMARY TRADELINES FOR SALE"
Some sellers offer to make an account appear on your report as if you opened it when you didn't. That is fraud, and the person who signs the application carries the risk. We won't do it or refer you to anyone who does. If someone pitches it to you, send us the pitch.

CREDIT BUILDER LOANS
How they work: a small amount is held in a savings account while you make monthly payments; at the end you receive the money. The lender reports an installment loan with your payment history.
Why they help: payment history is the biggest part of a score, and many people have no installment history at all.
What to check: that it reports to all three bureaus, the monthly cost and any fees. Many credit unions offer them.

SECURED CREDIT CARDS
How they work: you put down a deposit that becomes your limit; the card works and reports like a normal card. Some convert to unsecured later and return the deposit.
What to look for: reports to all three bureaus, no annual fee, a path to unsecured. Some check credit and some don't, so ask us before applying while your rounds run.

THREE RULES FOR ANY ACCOUNT
1. Confirm it reports to the bureaus before you pay anyone. Non-reporting products don't build credit.
2. Never carry a balance you can't pay off.
3. Set up autopay. One late payment can undo months of progress.

YOUR TAKEAWAY
Before opening anything, write down which bureaus it reports to and what it costs, and check with us first.
