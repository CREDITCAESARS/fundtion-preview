The 5 sections of a credit report

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
What the five sections of a credit report are, and what to look for in each one.

Most people look only at the score. The score is the last page of a book. Your report has five sections, and once you know them, our updates will make sense.

SECTION ONE: PERSONAL INFORMATION
Your name and name variations, current and former addresses, date of birth, a partial Social Security number and employers.
Why it matters: this is how a bureau decides what belongs to you. An address you never lived at, or a name variation that isn't yours, can be how someone else's account ended up on your file. It's also how files get mixed with a relative who shares your name. We review this section first.

SECTION TWO: ACCOUNTS (ALSO CALLED TRADELINES)
Every credit account reported about you, open and closed, positive and negative: cards, auto loans, mortgages, student loans, personal loans, store cards, collections and charge-offs. The next lesson walks through one account field by field.

SECTION THREE: PUBLIC RECORDS
Today this is mainly bankruptcy. Civil judgments and tax liens stopped appearing on consumer credit reports in 2017 and 2018. If you still see one, tell us. A Chapter 7 bankruptcy can report for up to 10 years from filing; a Chapter 13 is typically 7 years.

SECTION FOUR: INQUIRIES
A hard inquiry means you applied for credit. A soft inquiry means someone reviewed your file, or you checked your own. Only hard inquiries affect your score, and only hard inquiries are visible to lenders.

SECTION FIVE: CONSUMER STATEMENTS AND DISPUTE FLAGS
Any statement you added, and any account currently marked as disputed.
Important: an open dispute flag can stop an automated loan approval. That's one reason not to apply for a mortgage or other major loan while your dispute rounds are running. Ask us first.

YOUR TAKEAWAY
Open your report and find all five sections before the next lesson.
