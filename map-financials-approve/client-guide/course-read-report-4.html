Inquiries, public records and personal info

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
How inquiries work, what can appear as a public record, and why the personal information section deserves a careful read.

HARD AND SOFT INQUIRIES
A hard inquiry means you applied for credit. It stays on your report for 2 years and affects FICO scores for 12 months. A soft inquiry (pre-approvals, account reviews, checking your own credit) has no score impact and isn't visible to lenders.

RATE SHOPPING
Several hard inquiries for the same kind of loan (auto, mortgage or student loan) within a short window are counted as one. Depending on the scoring model, that window is 14 to 45 days. This does NOT apply to credit cards: five card applications are five inquiries.

INQUIRIES YOU DON'T RECOGNIZE
An inquiry from a lender you never applied to can be disputed, and it can be an early sign of identity theft. Tell us right away if you see one.

PUBLIC RECORDS
Civil judgments and tax liens should no longer appear on consumer credit reports. Bankruptcy still does. If you see a judgment or a lien, tell us.

PERSONAL INFORMATION
Read every line: addresses you never lived at, a different middle name, an employer you never worked for, or a Social Security number variation. Each one can be how another person's data attaches to your file. Correcting this section is part of the first steps of your program.

YOUR TAKEAWAY
Read your personal information section line by line. Write down anything that isn't yours and email it to admin@mapfinancials.com (never post it in the community).
