The dates that matter

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
The five dates on an account, and the one that controls how long a negative item can report.

DATE OPENED
When the account started. It feeds the average age of your accounts.

DATE OF LAST ACTIVITY
The last time anything happened on the account.

DATE OF LAST PAYMENT
The last payment received. If a collector shows a last-payment date you don't recognize, it may be an error.

DATE REPORTED (LAST UPDATED)
The last time the company sent data to the bureau.

DATE OF FIRST DELINQUENCY (DOFD): THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST
Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, most negative items can report for 7 years plus 180 days from the date of first delinquency: the date you first fell behind on the ORIGINAL account and never caught up.
That clock is tied to the original delinquency. It does not restart when the debt is sold to a collector, sold again, paid, or updated.

WHAT RE-AGING MEANS
Re-aging is reporting a later first-delinquency date than the real one, which would keep an item on your report longer than the law allows. A collection's first-delinquency date should match the original account's date. When they don't match, that is inaccurate reporting worth looking at.

ONE MORE THING
Paying a collection does not change its first-delinquency date or the date it must come off. It changes the balance to zero and the status to paid. Ask us before paying any collection while your rounds are running.

YOUR TAKEAWAY
For each collection or charge-off on your report, write down the date of first delinquency. If a collection's date doesn't match the original account's date, send us a note at admin@mapfinancials.com.
