Utilization and statement dates

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
What utilization is, why the statement date matters, and the targets to aim for.

WHAT IT IS
Utilization is your reported card balance divided by your credit limit. It's measured per card and across all cards combined, and both count.

THE PART MOST PEOPLE MISS
Scores use the balance your card company REPORTS, which is usually your STATEMENT balance, on the statement closing date. Not the due date. So you can pay in full every month and still report a high balance if you pay after the statement closes.

HOW TO MANAGE IT
1. Find each card's statement closing date (in the app, or call and ask).
2. Pay the balance down a few days BEFORE that closing date.
3. Let a small balance report, roughly 1 to 9 percent of the limit.
4. Pay the rest after it reports.

TARGETS
Under 30 percent: you've stopped the damage. Under 10 percent: good. A small balance on one card and zero on the others is the commonly recommended setup (sometimes called All Zero Except One).

RAISING THE LIMIT
A higher limit lowers utilization without paying anything. Some card issuers review limit increases with a soft check. Ask whether it's a soft or hard check, and ask us before requesting one while your rounds run.

CHECK FOR A MISSING LIMIT
If a card reports a balance with a zero or blank limit, some scoring models treat it as maxed out. Tell us if you see one.

WHY THIS MATTERS
Utilization is based on your current reported balances, so it reflects changes quickly. It's the part of your file you control month to month.

YOUR TAKEAWAY
Write down the statement closing date for every card you have, and set a reminder a few days before each one.
