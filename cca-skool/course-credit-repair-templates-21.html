🚨 Block & Deleted Method

Block and Deleted method

15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2 (often referred to as FCRA Section 605B) mandates that consumer reporting agencies (CRAs) block reporting of information stemming from alleged identity theft within 4 business days of receiving proper documentation. This applies to accounts, bills, or inquiries the consumer identifies as fraudulent, ensuring, with an identity theft report, that such data does not harm their creditworthiness.

3. Charge-Offs

📌 FCRA § 623(a)(1)(A) – A furnisher (creditor) cannot report information that it knows or should know is inaccurate.

👉 Use this against charge-offs reported with wrong balances, dates, or continued updating after being sold.

 

Charge Offs:

👉 Violation of 15 U.S. Code § 1635 right of recission

👉 15 U.S. Code § 1692g Validation of Debts, the debt collector must cease collection of any alleged debt owed until verification of the alleged debt or a copy of a judgment or the name and address of the original creditor.

⸻

4. Bankruptcies

📌 FCRA § 607(b) – Credit reporting agencies must follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy.

👉 Since CRAs don’t get bankruptcy info directly from courts (they buy it from third-party vendors like LexisNexis), you can challenge accuracy, source, and verification.

⸻

5. Evictions (Public Records / LexisNexis Reporting)

📌 FCRA § 611(a) – You have the right to dispute inaccurate or unverifiable information, and CRAs must reinvestigate within 30 days.

👉 Use this when disputing eviction records pulled from LexisNexis or other third-party databases that may be outdated or unverifiable.


👉 15 U.S. Code § 1681s-2 (a)(3): You did not enter this notice of dispute; you had 30 days from date you have received my 1st dispute and because you didn’t enter it your company is in violation

👉 15 U.S. Code § 1681e(b) – Compliance Procedures Maximum Possible Accuracy
